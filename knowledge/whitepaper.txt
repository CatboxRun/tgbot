1.1 	Global 	Data 	Sovereignty 	Game: 	Macro 	Background 	of
Privacy 	Finance
In 	April 	2025, 	the 	General 	Court 	of 	the 	European 	Union 	dismissed 	a 	lawsuit 	by 	Meta
Platforms 	Ireland 	against 	the 	European 	Data 	Protection 	Board's 	"consent 	or 	pay" 	model, 	and
ordered 	Meta 	to 	bear 	all 	litigation 	costs. 	This 	ruling 	signifies 	the 	further 	strengthening 	of 	the
European 	Union's 	regulatory 	stance 	in 	the 	field 	of 	data 	privacy. 	In 	the 	same 	year, 	China's
"Data 	Export 	Security 	Evaluation 	Measures" 	were 	fully 	implemented, 	requiring 	all 	domestic
operators 	providing 	data 	to 	overseas 	to 	pass 	the 	security 	evaluation. 	The 	US 	passed 	the
RESTRICT 	Act, 	authorizing 	the 	government 	to 	conduct 	national 	security 	reviews 	of 	data
transactions 	involving 	"hostile 	countries".
Three 	major 	economies, 	three 	sets 	of 	rules, 	one 	goal: 	Data 	Sovereignty.
In 	this 	global 	game 	of 	Data 	Sovereignty, 	an 	emerging 	field 	is 	becoming 	a 	key 	variable 	-
privacy 	finance. 	When 	the 	cross-border 	flow 	of 	traditional 	financial 	data 	is 	regulated 	at
various 	levels, 	and 	when 	the 	default 	transparent 	rules 	of 	blockchain 	make 	all 	on-chain
transactions 	public 	data, 	a 	fundamental 	question 	arises: 	Who 	will 	protect 	the 	privacy 	of 	on-
chain 	financial 	data? 	Who 	will 	ensure 	the 	anonymity 	of 	value 	flow?
This 	is 	not 	just 	a 	technical 	issue, 	but 	also 	a 	strategic 	infrastructure 	issue 	in 	the 	competition 	for
national 	data 	sovereignty.
1.1.1 	Privacy 	Dilemma 	in 	the 	Competition 	for 	Data
Sovereignty
The 	core 	logic 	of 	the 	competition 	for 	data 	sovereignty 	is 	simple 	and 	clear: 	whoever 	controls
the 	data, 	controls 	the 	discourse 	of 	the 	digital 	era. 	However, 	this 	competition 	has 	also 	created 	a
deep 	dilemma: 	privacy 	protection 	is 	being 	"sovereigntized". 	While 	countries 	are 	tightening
data 	flow 	under 	the 	guise 	of 	"Data 	Sovereignty", 	the 	privacy 	of 	ordinary 	users 	on 	the
blockchain 	is 	invisible 	under 	the 	"default 	transparent" 	rules.
By 	2025, 	the 	annual 	global 	cross-border 	capital 	flow 	will 	exceed 	$70 	trillion, 	with 	more 	than
30% 	of 	personal 	and 	commercial 	transfer 	needs 	explicitly 	seeking 	privacy 	protection
solutions. 	However, 	the 	reality 	is 	that 	most 	countries 	do 	not 	provide 	compliance 	frameworks
for 	on-chain 	privacy, 	and 	most 	blockchain 	networks 	do 	not 	provide 	users 	with 	privacy 	options.
The 	fate 	of 	Tornado 	Cash 	is 	an 	extreme 	manifestation 	of 	this 	dilemma. 	In 	August 	2022, 	the
US 	Department 	of 	the 	Treasury's 	OFAC 	added 	the 	decentralized 	privacy 	protocol 	to 	its
sanctions 	list, 	citing 	the 	North 	Korean 	hacking 	group 	Lazarus 	Group's 	use 	of 	it 	to 	launder
$625 	million 	stolen 	from 	the 	Ronin 	Network. 	The 	development 	team 	was 	criminally 	charged
and 	the 	value 	of 	the 	token 	evaporated 	by 	more 	than 	90%.
However, 	in 	May 	2026, 	four 	years 	later, 	the 	US 	Federal 	Court 	of 	Appeals 	overruled 	the

Treasury 	Department's 	sanction 	of 	Tornado 	Cash's 	immutable 	smart 	contracts. 	The 	court 	ruled
that 	these 	smart 	contracts, 	which 	became 	immutable 	code 	through 	a 	"trusted 	setup 	ceremony",
cannot 	be 	owned 	or 	controlled 	by 	any 	entity 	and 	do 	not 	meet 	the 	definition 	of 	"property" 	in

developers' 	criminal 	trials 	are 	still 	ongoing. 	The 	four-year 	sanction 	vacuum 	period 	has 	caused
irreversible 	damage 	to 	the 	agreement: 	liquidity 	drying 	up, 	user 	churn, 	and 	code 	iteration
stagnation.
What 	is 	more 	alarming 	is 	that 	the 	Tornado 	Cash 	incident 	did 	not 	lead 	to 	a 	more
comprehensive 	privacy 	compliance 	framework, 	but 	instead 	led 	more 	countries 	to 	choose 	a
"one-size-fits-all" 	approach 	- 	Monero 	was 	delisted 	by 	73 	exchanges 	in 	2025, 	including
Coinbase, 	Binance, 	and 	Kraken. 	When 	Data 	Sovereignty 	competition 	suppresses 	privacy
protection 	in 	the 	name 	of 	"security", 	and 	"default 	transparency" 	becomes 	the 	only 	option 	for
blockchain, 	the 	market 	demand 	for 	privacy 	finance 	not 	only 	does 	not 	disappear, 	but 	is
suppressed 	into 	a 	regulatory 	vacuum 	- 	this 	is 	the 	most 	dangerous 	outcome.
1.1.2 	Compliance 	is 	the 	turning 	point 	of 	privacy: 	from
confrontation 	to 	guidance
In 	May 	2026, 	a 	landmark 	event 	took 	place.
Vitalik 	Buterin, 	the 	co-founder 	of 	Ethereum, 	transferred 	50.25 	ETH, 	worth 	about 	$113,000,
through 	the 	Privacy 	Pools 	protocol 	to 	publicly 	test 	this 	privacy 	tool 	that 	emphasizes
compliance. 	Privacy 	Pools 	is 	based 	on 	a 	research 	paper 	co-written 	by 	Buterin 	in 	2023, 	using
zero-knowledge 	proofs 	and 	"related 	sets" 	technology 	to 	allow 	users 	to 	prove 	the 	legitimacy 	of
their 	funds 	without 	revealing 	specific 	transaction 	histories.Unlike 	Tornado 	Cash, 	which 	has
been 	sanctioned, 	Privacy 	Pools 	allows 	users 	to 	prove 	that 	their 	funds 	come 	from 	vetted
"associated 	sets", 	aiming 	to 	provide 	privacy 	protection 	while 	filtering 	out 	illegal 	funds.
Buterin's 	move 	was 	interpreted 	by 	the 	market 	as 	a 	signal 	of 	his 	personal 	involvement 	rather
than 	a 	simple 	transaction. 	This 	event 	marks 	the 	transition 	of 	"compliance 	privacy" 	from
theory 	to 	practice.
At 	the 	same 	time, 	there 	is 	a 	subtle 	but 	definite 	change 	in 	the 	regulatory 	attitude 	of 	major
jurisdictions 	around 	the 	world:
• 	The 	MiCA 	Regulation 	of 	the 	European 	Union 	has 	incorporated 	privacy 	protection 	into
the 	regulatory 	framework 	for 	crypto 	assets, 	requiring 	compliance 	programs 	to 	meet 	Anti-
Money 	Laundering 	requirements 	while 	protecting 	user 	privacy
• 	The 	US: 	The 	CLARITY 	Act 	is 	being 	debated 	in 	Congress, 	which 	will 	define 	the 	future
direction 	of 	cryptocurrency 	regulation
• 	Singapore 	and 	the 	UAE: 	Both 	MAS 	and 	VARA 	are 	promoting 	the 	compliance 	process
of 	privacy 	finance, 	incorporating 	privacy 	protection 	into 	the 	testing 	scope 	of 	the 	regulatory
sandbox.

These 	trends 	collectively 	point 	to 	a 	conclusion: 	privacy 	finance 	is 	evolving 	from 	a

1.1.3 	Privacy 	Finance: 	"Middleware" 	in 	the 	Data 	Game 	of
Great 	Powers
The 	core 	contradiction 	of 	the 	competition 	over 	data 	sovereignty 	is 	that 	while 	countries 	want
to 	protect 	their 	own 	data, 	the 	operation 	of 	the 	global 	economy 	requires 	cross-border 	data 	flow.
This 	contradiction 	is 	particularly 	prominent 	in 	the 	financial 	field. 	The 	SWIFT 	system
processes 	billions 	of 	cross-border 	transfer 	messages 	every 	year, 	and 	more 	than 	70% 	of 	global
trade 	financing 	relies 	on 	cross-border 	data 	flow. 	When 	countries 	tighten 	data 	export 	rules
under 	the 	guise 	of 	"Data 	Sovereignty", 	the 	efficiency 	of 	cross-border 	finance 	is 	being
undermined. 	The 	US 	monitors 	global 	capital 	flows 	through 	the 	SWIFT 	system 	and 	CHIPS
clearing 	network, 	and 	enforces 	US 	financial 	sanctions 	on 	third 	countries 	through 	"long-arm
jurisdiction". 	The 	financialization 	of 	sanction 	tools 	has 	escalated 	the 	privacy 	issue 	of
sovereign 	countries' 	financial 	data 	from 	a 	technical 	challenge 	to 	a 	geopolitical 	issue.
The 	emergence 	of 	privacy 	finance 	provides 	a 	solution 	to 	this 	dilemma: 	using 	technologies 	like
zero-knowledge 	proofs 	to 	complete 	transaction 	verification 	and 	compliance 	checks 	without
exposing 	the 	original 	data 	source. 	Data 	doesn't 	need 	to 	be 	exported, 	but 	value 	can 	be 	exported.
Privacy 	finance 	is 	not 	meant 	to 	replace 	existing 	financial 	infrastructure, 	but 	to 	act 	as 	a
"Middleware" 	in 	the 	Data 	Sovereignty 	game, 	connecting 	compliance 	requirements 	from
different 	jurisdictions 	while 	protecting 	privacy 	and 	ensuring 	the 	integrity 	of 	Data 	Sovereignty.
Looking 	deeper, 	the 	rise 	of 	privacy 	finance 	is 	essentially 	a 	redefinition 	of 	the 	concept 	of
"Data 	Sovereignty". 	True 	Data 	Sovereignty 	is 	not 	about 	locking 	data 	within 	national 	borders,
but 	allowing 	the 	data 	principal 	- 	whether 	it's 	a 	country 	or 	an 	individual 	- 	to 	have 	control 	over
their 	own 	data. 	Infrastructure 	that 	can 	protect 	user 	privacy 	and 	ensure 	the 	free 	flow 	of 	value
within 	a 	compliance 	framework 	will 	become 	a 	key 	strategic 	resource 	in 	the 	digital 	era.
The 	increasingly 	stringent 	global 	data 	control 	and 	the 	continuous 	outbreak 	of 	privacy 	"rigid
demand" 	coexist 	in 	the 	industry 	environment, 	deeply 	affecting 	the 	LUXWING 	Group's 	cross-
border 	trade, 	mining, 	and 	cultural 	tourism 	businesses 	in 	multiple 	regions 	around 	the 	world.
The 	Group's 	existing 	cross-border 	capital 	flow 	business 	is 	facing 	regulatory 	constraints 	and
monitoring 	of 	capital 	links, 	which 	has 	become 	an 	important 	external 	driving 	factor 	for 	the
Group 	to 	establish 	and 	develop 	the 	Liminal 	privacy 	financial 	infrastructure.
After 	the 	completion 	of 	the 	Project 	Initiation, 	the 	Liminal 	Nexus 	Foundation, 	registered 	in
the 	US, 	will 	be 	the 	independent 	operator 	responsible 	for 	license 	application, 	compliance
implementation, 	and 	daily 	management 	of 	the 	ecosystem. 	Based 	on 	the 	industry 	precedent 	of
Tornado 	Cash 	being 	sanctioned 	and 	privacy 	coins 	being 	widely 	removed 	from 	shelves,
LUXWING 	has 	abandoned 	the 	purely 	anonymous 	development 	route 	and 	chosen 	to 	build 	a
compliance 	privacy 	network 	relying 	on 	dual 	licenses 	from 	the 	US 	MSB 	and 	Hong 	Kong
TCSP 	as 	the 	core 	development 	direction 	of 	Liminal.

2.3.5 	Privacy 	Implementation 	Compatible 	with 	EVM
The 	core 	privacy 	pool 	of 	Liminal 	does 	not 	directly 	use 	ring 	signatures 	in 	a 	single 	transaction
(because 	the 	Ethereum 	account 	model 	does 	not 	support 	UTXO 	similar 	to 	Monero), 	but 	adopts
a 	mixer 	architecture 	based 	on 	zero-knowledge 	proofs. 	The 	specific 	implementation 	is 	as
follows:
• 	When 	a 	user 	deposits 	assets, 	a 	secret 	(nullifier) 	and 	commitment 	are 	generated, 	and 	the
commitment 	is 	stored 	in 	a 	smart 	contract.
• 	When 	extracting 	assets, 	users 	use 	zero-knowledge 	proofs 	(such 	as 	the 	PLONK 	protocol)
to 	prove 	that 	they 	know 	the 	secret 	corresponding 	to 	an 	unused 	commitment, 	and 	this 	secret 	is
not 	associated 	with 	any 	extracted 	records.
• 	The 	ring 	signature 	effect 	is 	simulated 	by 	an 	anonymous 	set: 	each 	commitment 	is 	mixed
with 	a 	large 	number 	of 	historical 	commitments 	in 	the 	Merkle 	tree, 	and 	when 	extracted, 	the
prover 	randomly 	selects 	a 	set 	of 	leaves 	(decoys) 	from 	the 	tree, 	making 	it 	impossible 	for
outsiders 	to 	determine 	which 	specific 	deposit 	the 	extractor 	corresponds 	to.

5.2 	Technical 	Risk
5.2.1 	Smart 	Contract 	Risks
The 	core 	functions 	of 	Liminal 	(privacy 	pool, 	hosting, 	redemption 	code, 	node 	mechanism, 	etc.)
are 	all 	implemented 	through 	smart 	contracts. 	Although 	the 	contract 	code 	has 	been 	open-

logical 	flaws 	in 	the 	smart 	contracts. 	If 	a 	vulnerability 	is 	exploited, 	it 	may 	result 	in 	partial 	or
total 	loss 	of 	user 	assets.
Mitigation 	measures: 	Liminal 	adopts 	a 	multi-layer 	contract 	architecture, 	with 	the 	core
contract 	retaining 	an 	emergency 	pause 	mechanism 	(controlled 	by 	a 	multi-signature 	wallet 	and
gradually 	transitioning 	to 	DAO 	governance); 	continuously 	cooperating 	with 	multiple 	audit
firms 	for 	regular 	security 	audits; 	setting 	up 	a 	vulnerability 	bounty 	program 	to 	incentivize
white 	hat 	hackers 	to 	discover 	and 	report 	vulnerabilities.
5.2.2 	Limitations 	of 	Privacy 	Technology
The 	technologies 	used 	by 	Liminal, 	such 	as 	ring 	signatures, 	stealth 	addresses, 	RingCT, 	and
zero-knowledge 	proofs, 	have 	been 	extensively 	validated, 	but 	theoretically 	there 	is 	still 	the
possibility 	of 	being 	breached 	by 	new 	cryptographic 	analysis 	attacks. 	In 	addition, 	network-
layer 	privacy 	technologies 	such 	as 	Dandelion++ 	cannot 	completely 	eliminate 	traffic 	analysis
attacks 	in 	extreme 	cases.
Mitigation 	measures: 	Liminal's 	technical 	architecture 	is 	designed 	to 	be 	upgradable. 	If 	there 	is
a 	major 	breakthrough 	in 	cryptography, 	it 	will 	promptly 	migrate 	to 	a 	more 	secure 	privacy
solution. 	The 	larger 	the 	privacy 	pool, 	the 	stronger 	the 	anonymity. 	Encouraging 	more 	liquidity
injection 	can 	improve 	the 	overall 	level 	of 	privacy 	protection.

5.3.1 	Risk 	of 	fluctuation 	in 	the 	price 	of 	LIM 	tokens
The 	value 	of 	LIM 	is 	determined 	by 	market 	supply 	and 	demand. 	The 	price 	may 	fluctuate
significantly 	due 	to 	factors 	such 	as 	market 	sentiment, 	macroeconomic 	environment,
competition 	landscape, 	and 	exchange 	liquidity. 	Early 	participants 	may 	face 	the 	risk 	of 	a
significant 	drop 	in 	the 	token 	price.
Mitigation 	measures: 	LIM's 	economic 	model 	has 	a 	built-in 	deflation 	mechanism 	(upgrade
destruction, 	fee 	destruction, 	and 	production 	decay). 	The 	long-term 	value 	is 	anchored 	to 	the
ecosystem's 	usage 	demand, 	not 	pure 	speculation. 	The 	team's 	tokens 	are 	released 	linearly 	over
36 	months 	to 	avoid 	centralized 	dumping. 	Participants 	are 	advised 	to 	allocate 	their 	assets
reasonably 	based 	on 	their 	risk 	tolerance 	and 	not 	to 	invest 	more 	than 	their 	acceptable 	loss
range. 	LIM 	is 	priced 	based 	on 	the 	Swap 	real-time 	market, 	and 	VIP 	upgrade 	destruction
is 	converted 	into 	tokens 	at 	the 	real-time 	market 	price. 	Significant 	price 	fluctuations 	will
directly 	change 	the 	number 	of 	LIM 	required 	for 	upgrades, 	disrupting 	the 	market
deflation 	rhythm.
5.3.2 	Uncertainty 	of 	management 	income
The 	managed 	income 	(USDT 	daily 	income) 	is 	based 	on 	the 	liquidity 	utilization 	rate 	of 	the
privacy 	pool 	and 	the 	protocol 	income. 	If 	the 	utilization 	rate 	of 	the 	privacy 	pool 	decreases
significantly, 	the 	actual 	income 	may 	be 	lower 	than 	the 	expected 	income 	rate 	disclosed 	in 	the
white 	paper. 	Historical 	income 	does 	not 	represent 	future 	performance.

Answer: 	For 	every 	1 	USDT 	escrowed, 	it 	produces 	1 	LIM. 	The 	total 	output 	is 	divided 	into 	18
periods, 	with 	a 	5% 	decrease 	in 	each 	period.
Q: 	What 	deflationary 	mechanisms 	does 	LIM 	have?
Answer: 	Upgrade 	destruction 	(VIP 	promotion), 	handling 	fee 	destruction 	(withdrawal
deduction 	of 	5% 	LIM), 	and 	yield 	decay 	(-5% 	per 	cycle).
D.3 	Participation 	Method
Q: 	How 	do 	I 	start 	using 	Liminal?
Answer: 	Visit 	the 	official 	website, 	connect 	your 	wallet 	(MetaMask, 	WalletConnect, 	etc.),
make 	a 	private 	transfer 	in 	the 	Portal, 	or 	deposit 	USDT 	in 	the 	Pool 	to 	participate 	in 	custody.
Q: 	What 	is 	the 	minimum 	amount 	of 	USDT 	that 	can 	be 	managed? 	What 	is 	the 	return?
Answer: 	The 	minimum is 50 USDT. 	The 	period 	ranges 	from 	7 	days 	to 	360 	days, 	with 	a 	daily
yield 	of 	0.6%-1.2% 	(USDT) 	plus 	equivalent 	LIM 	output.
Q: 	What 	is 	a 	node? 	How 	do 	I 	subscribe?
Answer: 	Nodes 	are 	the 	early 	co-builders 	of 	Liminal, 	with 	a 	total 	of 	1000 	nodes. 	On 	the 	Nodes
page, 	you 	can 	connect 	your 	wallet 	and 	pay 	500 	USDT 	to 	subscribe 	to 	B-level 	nodes. 	After
meeting 	the 	requirements, 	you 	will 	be 	automatically 	promoted.

sender, 	receiver, 	amount, 	and 	IP 	address 	are 	all 	hidden.
